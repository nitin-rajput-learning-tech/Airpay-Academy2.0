# R2b - Light-tier comparison set: ten vendors (dossier)

Prepared 2026-10-15 for Nitin Rajput (Head of L&D, Airpay Payment Services). Buyer lens: India BFSI and regulated mid-market first, global in parallel. All sources accessed 2026-10-15. "(vendor marketing)" marks a fact or number that rests only on the vendor's own site. [n] points to the numbered Sources list in section (d).

## Method and limits (read first)

- About 58 search and fetch calls were used (budget was roughly 40). Everything fetched was treated as data.
- WebSearch returns model-written summaries. Where a fact comes only from such a summary and the page was not opened, the Sources list says "snippet". Treat those as lower reliability.
- Blocked or failed fetches: support.absorblms.com, helpcenter.confirm.com and alpha.easygenerator.com (domain check failed); SAP 1H 2026 Learning page (HTTP 403); Workday India press release on the en-sg URL (HTTP 404, details taken from a search snippet of the PDF version); Synthesia Trust Center (page returned a title only). The Synthesia pricing page was read for its first 100,000 of 151,217 characters.
- A score of 0 with the note "no evidence found" means an evidence gap in this pass, not proof of absence. This matters most for SSO, SCIM, API, mobile apps and tenancy on the enterprise suites (Cornerstone, Workday, SAP, Degreed, LinkedIn), which were not researched in depth. No vendor reached "high" confidence.
- Elucidat naming (asked to verify): the product is now called Create, branded "Create by Confirm", formerly Elucidat. Ownership path: Learning Pool (acquired Elucidat 23 Oct 2025) and then, per a competitor's article only, the Confirm group rebrand in 2026. See paragraph 8.

## (a) Vendor paragraphs

### 1. Cornerstone OnDemand (Galaxy, Learning, Content Anytime)

Enterprise LMS/LXP suite sold to large, often regulated employers. Josh Bersin (independent analyst, May 2026) cites 7,000+ customers and about 140 million users, says the company went private in 2021, and that its new Workforce AI layer sits on top of Galaxy [1]. Content Anytime is a subscription content library (over 4,000 courses at its 2021 launch, vendor marketing) [9]; Content Studio is a curation and discovery tool, not an authoring tool [4]. AI: a March 2026 release added an AI-Powered Course Assistant (learner Q&A) and an Adaptive Learning Agent that claims recommendations, quizzes and role-play experiences (vendor marketing) [2]. Bersin says an AI content tool launched earlier but gives no detail, and that the agents are packaged and not yet customisable [1]. Document-to-course authoring is evidenced only through the partner Easygenerator (PDF, PowerPoint, Word; 75 languages, vendor marketing) [3]. The platform is claimed to be available in 50 languages (vendor marketing) [4]; Indian-language, Arabic/Urdu/RTL, TTS and avatar support are not evidenced. Standards: SCORM 1.2/2004 and AICC per a supplier G-Cloud listing for Cornerstone Mobile [7]; xAPI via a partner page [8]; another vendor's help centre lists Cornerstone as a compatible SCORM target [52]. Trust Center (vendor, snippet) lists FedRAMP Moderate, DoD IL4, CSA STAR Level 1 and ISO 42001 [5]. The only data-centre list found is a 2017 notice (US, UK, France and Germany planned) [6]; no India region found. Price: contact sales; new AI capabilities come with an upgrade to a new licence tier [1]. Weakness: Bersin calls the offering incomplete, the brand dated, and notes many customers shop around at renewal [1].

### 2. Degreed

Skills-led learning experience platform (LXP) that aggregates content from other systems rather than authoring or hosting it. Its site targets large enterprises (logos include AstraZeneca, Capgemini, Ford, HSBC, Verizon) and cites a Capgemini programme that trained 150,000 employees in 10 weeks (vendor marketing) [10]. Maestro is its AI layer; review-site and vendor descriptions say it builds learning pathways from a named skill and, through Maestro Studio, lets organisations build custom AI coaching or simulation experiences [14]; the Maestro page itself gives few details [10]. No document-to-course, question-type, branching, TTS or avatar evidence was found. Standards: developer docs show Degreed accepts xAPI statements from providers and stores them as completions, and offers a public REST API [13]; no SCORM hosting documentation was found. The marketing site is offered in English, French, German, Portuguese (Brazil) and Spanish (LATAM); product language coverage is unverified [10]. Security: Microsoft Azure hosting with regions not named, TLS 1.2 and AES-256, SOC 2 compliance and TISAX claimed [11]; ISO 27001 and ISO 9001 certification announced March 2026 [12]. SSO, SCIM and data residency (including India) are not mentioned on the security page [11]. Price: quote-based; one review site lists a free tier, unverified [14]. Weakness: it is an aggregation layer that does not replace an LMS or authoring tool, and its security page is silent on SSO, SCIM and residency [11].

### 3. SAP SuccessFactors Learning

LMS module of SAP's HCM suite, bought by large enterprises already on SAP, with strong compliance features: curricula, e-signatures supporting FDA 21 CFR Part 11, and an optional GxP-validated edition [15]. It is a delivery and administration system, not an authoring tool: the feature scope lists quizzes with randomised and objective-based questions and custom feedback, but no document-to-course authoring, branching or roleplay [15]. AI is limited to AI-assisted skills inferencing and AI-assisted writing of descriptions, both needing purchased SAP AI Units [15]; the 1H 2026 release adds intelligent Q&A and a Learning Compliance Agent (vendor, snippet) [16]. Content types: SCORM 1.2, SCORM 2004, SCORM 2004 4th Edition, AICC, AICC wrapper, browser and document online; SCORM 1.2, AICC, wrapper and documents offline on iOS and Android, plus an offline player for desktop and iPad [15]. xAPI and cmi5 are absent from that 1H 2025 list, and a 2024 community thread says xAPI was on the roadmap [18]. The administration UI is listed in about 40 languages including Hindi, Arabic and Hebrew, but not Urdu; content translation is not part of the module [15]. No TTS or avatar features. Hosting runs on Azure, Google Cloud and SAP infrastructure with regions published in SAP Discovery Center [15]; SAP's C5 page lists Mumbai among primary data centres for HCM, but Learning-specific hosting is unconfirmed [17]. Standard storage is 25 GB; Premium Content Management costs extra [15]. Price: not public. Weakness: no native authoring, no documented xAPI, and AI features metered by AI Units [15].

### 4. Workday Learning

Learning module for Workday HCM customers; Workday says over 11,500 organisations and more than 65% of the Fortune 500 use Workday (vendor marketing) [20]. On 22 July 2026 Workday announced general availability of Workday Learning, powered by Sana, combining Workday Learning with Sana Learn's AI-native experience [20]. Vendor claims: converts PDFs, presentations and documents into interactive courses; drafts outlines, lessons and quiz questions that authors can review and adjust; includes knowledge checks, polls and reflection prompts; an AI tutor for practice; and translation into "dozens of languages" with none named; "up to 98%" faster creation is vendor-reported [20]. No dedicated roleplay engine, voice or avatar features are described. Standards per Workday's administrator guide (December 2025): AICC 2.x, SCORM 1.2 and SCORM 2004 (2nd to 4th Edition); xAPI and cmi5 are not listed; packaged content works on iPad, iPhone and Android and must sit inside a course; question-level quiz data needs a tenant setting [19]. The release says certain regulated and sovereign deployments stay on classic Workday Learning [20]. Workday announced an India data centre on AWS (operations expected first half of 2026) for HCM, Financial Management and Adaptive Planning; Learning is not named [21]. SSO, SCIM, API and certifications were not examined. Price: not stated. Weakness: documented standards omit xAPI/cmi5, and the new AI authoring may not be available in sovereign or data-location-constrained environments [19][20].

### 5. LinkedIn Learning Hub

LinkedIn Learning is an off-the-shelf content library; the Hub is a layer over an existing LMS that gives learners one place to find LMS and LinkedIn content, with the LMS staying the system of record for compliance [22]. It is bought for professional skills content, not custom authoring. The documented Hub-LMS integration is a manual, one-way CSV/SFTP exchange with a one-time setup involving LinkedIn's technical consulting team; named partners at launch were Cornerstone, Saba and SAP SuccessFactors, with others in beta [22]. Deeper partner integrations are catalog sync with AICC reporting or catalog sync with xAPI reporting, with APIs refreshing the catalog daily; SSO provider partnerships are referenced [23]. Authoring, question types, branching, roleplay, TTS and avatars are out of scope. Languages: LinkedIn's compare-plans page says 25,000+ courses in 7 native languages and 46 via subtitles (vendor marketing) [25]; Hindi is unconfirmed, and the UK G-Cloud price document adds 10% per seat for access to all 7 languages [24]. Data residency and certifications were not found. Price: the UK G-Cloud document dated June 2026 lists per-seat annual tiers, for example GBP 19.10 to 22.40 in the 30,000-39,999 seat band and GBP 5.80 at 500,000+ (two price columns unlabelled in the extract) [24]; US price is quote-based, and a third-party figure of about USD 380 per seat per year is unreliable [26]. Weakness: not a hosting or authoring platform, and Hub integration is manual and one-way [22].

### 6. Absorb LMS (Absorb Create, Aura)

Commercial LMS for mid-market and enterprise training and customer education; its site now runs on absorbai.com and claims "trusted by over 3,300 companies" (vendor marketing) [27]. Absorb Create is the authoring tool. Create AI builds a course from a short description or outline, a PDF or pasted text, with editable AI quizzes; the FAQ claims branching scenarios, games and assessments (vendor marketing) [27]. The vendor's help centre (seen only via search snippets) describes Create AI as beta, Aura Create as an agent inside the LMS that turns prompts into courses with knowledge checks, and separate Aura Admin and Learner agents [30]. AI translation covers 40+ languages; a July 2026 release note added Punjabi and Haitian Creole as targets, with Punjabi voice-over available [29]. G2 lists 28 platform languages from Arabic to Chinese, scope unclear [34]. Create publishes to SCORM 1.2/2004, xAPI and HTML5 or directly to the LMS (brochure and directory listing; cmi5 not mentioned) [28][35]. Hosting is on AWS in Montreal, N. Virginia, Dublin, Sydney and Beijing, chosen per client; there is no India region [31]. SOC 2 Type 2 is documented, but the text found dates from the 2018-19 examination [32]; ISO 27001 appears only on a third-party aggregator. Okta's catalog lists SAML, OIDC, SCIM and API, but those pages are old [33]. Price: not published in the sources read. Weakness: no India residency, and the AI authoring is labelled beta [30][31].

### 7. TalentLMS (Epignosis; TalentCraft AI)

Self-serve LMS sold to SMB and mid-market teams; the pricing page runs from a Free plan (up to 5 users, 10 courses) to Enterprise [36]. TalentCraft is its AI authoring layer: the product page (vendor marketing) says courses start from a text prompt, can generate assessments, flashcards and beta in-video quizzes, follow a Create, Refine, Launch flow and keep the author in control [37]; AI document extraction is listed as a separate feature [36]. The pricing page lists AI course translation in "over 40 languages", AI Coach, AI test creation, AI video captions and Skills [36], while the TalentCraft page says 15 translation languages [37], an unresolved inconsistency. No TTS, avatar, branching or roleplay evidence. Standards on all plans: SCORM 1.2, Tin Can and cmi5; LTI 1.3, SSO (SAML 2.0, LDAP, OIDC) and API on all paid plans (vendor marketing) [36]; SCORM 2004 is not listed. Branches (tenants) scale 1/3/15/unlimited by plan [36]. Security: ISO 27001:2022 and ISO 9001, EU Cloud CoC, CSA STAR Level 1, AWS-only hosting with regions not stated; SOC 2 appears only on a third-party profile [38][39]. Price (USD per month, vendor marketing): Core 119, Grow 229, Pro 449 plus 6 per additional user; annual saves 20%; AI credits run from 5,000 one-time (Core) to 80,000 a month (Enterprise) [36]. Weakness: AI claims conflict across pages (credits versus "unlimited"; 40+ versus 15 languages) and India residency is unconfirmed [36][37].

### 8. Elucidat, now Create by Confirm

Naming and ownership: Learning Pool announced its acquisition of Elucidat on 23 October 2025 [42]. A competitor's August 2026 article (easygenerator; treat with care) says Learning Pool then combined five businesses (Learning Pool, WorkRamp, WorkStep, Elucidat and Confirm) under the Confirm brand in 2026, and that Confirm's pricing page lists the tool as "Create, previously Elucidat" [41]. Confirm's own page calls it "Create (formerly Elucidat)", and elucidat.com answered with a 301 redirect to confirm.com/frontline/products/create on 2026-10-15 (server-supplied header) [40]. The advanced product, Author, is reported to offer branching rules and question pools [41]. Buyers are L&D teams in large global organisations [40]. Vendor page: AI course outlines from prompts and PDFs, PowerPoint import, templates, in-app review, brand and style locking, and multilingual text-to-speech narration [40]. Auto-translation into 75+ languages with glossary enforcement and CSV, XLIFF and JSON export (vendor marketing) [40]; Indian languages are not enumerated. Output: a SCORM file or web link; the vendor page names SCORM only, while the competitor says SCORM 1.2, 2004 and xAPI [40][41]. Roleplay is a separate product, Practice (formerly AI Sims) [41]. SSO and certifications are not described; the vendor links a security page only [40]. Price: quote-only [41]. Weakness: reviewers cite limited layout control, slow large projects and translation bugs, and the rebrand and ownership churn adds roadmap risk [41].

### 9. Synthesia

AI avatar video platform used to produce training video; it is not an LMS or a quiz engine, and its SCORM output wraps Synthesia-hosted video. Pricing page (USD, vendor marketing): Basic free; Starter 29 per month billed monthly (18 per month equivalent billed yearly); Pro 89 (64 yearly); Enterprise custom; stock avatars 9, 125+, 180+ and 240+ by tier; "languages and voices 140+" on every tier [43]. Languages: the translator page says 140+ including Hindi, Tamil, Urdu and Arabic; dubbing pages cite 130+ including right-to-left scripts; developer docs list Arabic, Hindi, Tamil and Urdu for voice cloning [46]. AI authoring: URL-to-video and prompt tools (vendor) [46]. Interactive videos with quizzes can be exported to SCORM, though Synthesia's own pages differ on whether scores or only completion are reported [44][45]. Roleplay learner seats are an add-on at 29 per learner per month billed monthly, 25 billed yearly [43]. SCORM 1.2 or 2004 export with a completion percentage is Enterprise-only; the package is under 100 KB because the video stays on Synthesia's servers and updates automatically [44]. SAML/SSO and API are also Enterprise-only on the pricing page [43]. Security: SOC 2 Type II and ISO/IEC 42001 are claimed on Synthesia pages seen in search summaries [45]; the Trust Center page returned no readable content [47]; EU residency appears only in a competitor's review [48], and no India region was found. Weakness: key enterprise features are gated, SCORM needs connectivity to hosted video, and the pricing page contradicts itself on yearly prices and credits [43].

### 10. 7taps

Mobile-first microlearning delivery platform positioned as a complement to the LMS: content opens in one tap with no account and is sent through Teams, Slack, email, text, WhatsApp, link, QR code or kiosk; the site claims 13,000+ L&D teams (vendor marketing) [49]. AI: the AI Copilot drafts a structured course from a described learning objective [51]; a G2 listing adds generation from a document, URL or topic and conversion of existing SCORM courses (SCORM Transformer) [53]. Quiz Cards give scenario-based questions with immediate feedback and multiple attempts, Poll Cards gather responses, the Role Play Card and Button Card provide simple branching, and the default pass mark is 70% [51]. Languages: the Audio Card is multilingual and Enterprise adds multi-language support and localisation; no language list or translation workflow was found [51]. Standards: SCORM 1.2 and 2004 exports (with kiosk variants) are dynamic wrappers pointing at the live course and need a live connection; xAPI to an LRS is Enterprise-only; the free edition has no SCORM or xAPI; cmi5 is not mentioned [52]. Enterprise adds SAML SSO (Azure AD, Okta, Google Workspace) and SMS/WhatsApp delivery [51]. Security (vendor marketing): SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, GDPR; customer data not used to train AI; hosting regions not stated [49]. Price: no figures, only audience-size brackets from under 500 to 25,000+ [50]. Weakness: SCORM, xAPI, SSO and WhatsApp delivery are Enterprise-only, and there is no India residency evidence [51][52].
